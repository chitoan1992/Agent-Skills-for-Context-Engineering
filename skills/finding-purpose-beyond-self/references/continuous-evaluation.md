# Continuous Evaluation & Iteration: From 30-Day Cycles to an Ultimate Purpose

> The 30-day workbook is the tactic. This is the strategy: a loop that
> evaluates and improves the plan itself, turning short cycles into a
> multi-year arc toward a purpose worth a life.

Purpose is not discovered once and held forever. It is a **hypothesis you
keep testing**. Each 30-day act of service produces evidence; this framework
turns that evidence into a refined direction, cycle after cycle, until the
small acts compound into something you'd call an ultimate purpose.

---

## The Loop (PDCA for purpose)

```
        ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
        │                                              │
   PLAN ──► DO ──► CHECK ──► ACT ──┐                    │
  (write a  (run a  (score   (refine                    │
  purpose   30-day  against  hypothesis,                │
  hypothesis cycle)  rubric)  scale or pivot) ──────────┘
  + one act)
```

Every cycle you do not just ask "did I keep the practice?" — you ask
"**did this practice make my purpose hypothesis more true, or did it falsify it?**"
A falsified hypothesis is a win: you spent 30 days, not 30 years, finding out.

---

## 1. PLAN — Write a Purpose Hypothesis

A hypothesis is falsifiable and specific. Not "I want to help people" but:

> "I believe my purpose involves **[verb from my usefulness pattern]** for
> **[specific group/person]**, because when I do it I feel **[X]** and it
> produces **[concrete benefit for them]**."

Example: *"I believe my purpose involves teaching engineers who lack mentors,
because when I do it I feel most alive and it visibly unblocks their careers."*

Then attach ONE 30-day act of service that tests it (see the workbook's
Commitment Phase). The act is the experiment; the hypothesis is what it tests.

---

## 2. DO — Run the 30-Day Cycle

Execute the daily act + two-line journal. No analysis mid-cycle; data
collection only. (Mid-cycle doubt is handled by re-reading your named person,
not by abandoning the experiment.)

---

## 3. CHECK — Score Against the Rubric

At day 30, score the cycle 1–5 on each dimension. This replaces "how did it
feel?" vibes with evidence you can compare across cycles.

| Dimension | 1 (weak) | 5 (strong) | Question to ask |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Energy** | Drained me | Energized me, even when tired | Did doing it give more than it took? |
| **Service** | No visible effect on anyone | Someone was measurably better off | Can I point to what changed for them? |
| **Sustainability** | Dreaded it, forced it | Wanted to continue past day 30 | Could I do this for a year? |
| **Identity fit** | Felt like a costume | Felt like the truest version of me | Did it feel like *me*, or like who I think I should be? |
| **Growth** | Stagnant, rote | Stretched and grew me | Am I becoming more capable / deeper? |

**Total / 25.** Track it. The number matters less than its *trend across cycles*.

---

## 4. ACT — Refine, Scale, or Pivot

Read the scores and choose ONE move for the next cycle:

- **Score 20–25 → SCALE.** The hypothesis is holding. Increase the
  commitment (more time, more people, higher stakes) or lengthen the horizon
  to 90 days. This is how a 30-min/day act grows toward a life's work.
- **Score 12–19 → REFINE.** Partly right. Change one variable — the recipient,
  the specific act, the time of day — and rerun. Note which dimension scored
  lowest; that names what to fix.
- **Score below 12 → PIVOT.** The hypothesis is likely false. Return to the
  workbook's Excavation Phase, pick a different usefulness pattern or pain,
  write a new hypothesis. **This is not failure; it is the fastest possible
  route to truth.** Most people never run the experiment at all.

Always rewrite the purpose hypothesis at the end of a cycle, even if only to
sharpen one word. The hypothesis evolving *is* the search succeeding.

---

## Review Cadence

Different horizons need different review depths. Set calendar reminders.

| Cadence | Duration | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| **Daily** | 1 min | Two-line journal after the act |
| **Weekly** | 5 min | Re-read the week's journal; adjust nothing, just notice |
| **Monthly** | 30 min | Score the rubric, run ACT, rewrite the hypothesis |
| **Quarterly** | 1 hr | Read the last 3 hypotheses side by side — are they converging? |
| **Yearly** | half a day | Write the year's "arc statement" (below) |

---

## The Purpose Ladder (small acts → ultimate purpose)

Ultimate purpose is not chosen at the top; it is **climbed to**. Each rung is
a completed cycle whose hypothesis held.

```
Year 3+ : Ultimate purpose — a direction you'd defend with your time for life
   ▲
Year 1–2: A through-line — the same pattern showing up across many cycles
   ▲
Quarter : A confirmed cause — 3 cycles pointing the same way
   ▲
Month   : A confirmed act — one cycle, score ≥ 20
   ▲
Day 1   : A hypothesis — a guess worth 30 days
```

You don't need to see the top of the ladder to climb the next rung. The
"ultimate" emerges from the pattern of what kept scoring high, not from a
single moment of revelation.

**Arc statement (write yearly):** *"Across this year's cycles, the through-line
was ______. The version of it I'm willing to give the next year to is ______."*

---

## Anti-Patterns for the Loop Itself

| Anti-pattern | Fix |
|---|---|
| Endlessly re-planning the hypothesis without running a cycle | Cap planning at one day; the experiment is the point |
| Pivoting every cycle, never scaling | A score 12–19 means REFINE (change one variable), not pivot |
| Treating the rubric score as a grade on your worth | It scores the *fit of the act*, never the person |
| Waiting for certainty before committing a year | Certainty is the *output* of cycles, not their prerequisite |
| Optimizing the framework instead of living it | If you've improved this doc twice without running a cycle, stop and serve someone today |

---

## How This Satisfies "Continuous Evaluate and Improve"

- **Evaluate**: the rubric (CHECK) turns each cycle into measured evidence.
- **Improve**: the ACT step refines the plan every single cycle; the cadence
  guarantees it never goes stale.
- **Continuous**: the ladder has no terminal rung — scaling a held hypothesis
  always opens a larger one. The loop is the destination.

The search is never "done," and that is the design: a purpose beyond yourself
is large enough that serving it better is always possible. The win condition is
not finishing the loop — it's being *in* it, on a rung that scores high, with a
named person better off because you showed up today.
